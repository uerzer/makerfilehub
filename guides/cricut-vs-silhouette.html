Cricut vs Silhouette: The Honest Comparison Before You Buy

You post in a crafting forum: "Cricut or Silhouette — which one should I get?" Within an hour you have forty replies and not one of them answers the question. Half the thread is Team Cricut telling you Design Space is amazing. The other half is Team Silhouette telling you Design Space is a walled garden. Nobody asks what you actually make.

That's the problem: the two machines are near-identical on paper, both cut vinyl, HTV, and cardstock with a drag blade, so the decision spirals into choice paralysis. You read spec sheets until the numbers blur, and the loudest voices in every thread are people defending a purchase they already made.

Here's why it matters more than it looks: you're not buying a machine, you're buying an ecosystem. Pick the wrong one and you'll fight the software on every single project, or pay a subscription you never budgeted for. The fix isn't another opinion. It's matching the machine to how you actually work, and the honest matrix below does exactly that.

Design Space vs Silhouette Studio: The Software Decides Most of It

Software is the real difference between these brands, and it's the part spec sheets bury.

Cricut's Design Space is a free cloud-based app that runs on phone, tablet, and desktop. Its biggest strength is the ready-made image library, the largest of any cutting brand, though a Cricut Access subscription (around $10/month or ~$96/year at the time of writing) unlocks most of it. The catch: Design Space needs an internet connection for most functions. If your craft room has weak Wi-Fi, that gets old fast.

Silhouette Studio is free desktop software for Windows and Mac with full design tools built in, no subscription required to design. You can draw, trace, weld, and lay out a project entirely offline, then cut over USB. Paid one-time upgrades add advanced tools: Designer Edition (roughly $30–50) and Business Edition (around $100). The trade-off: there's no phone or tablet app. If you design on an iPad at the kitchen table, Silhouette's desktop-only workflow doesn't fit.

One honest note on long-term cost: if you'd use Cricut Access heavily, Silhouette's one-time upgrades work out cheaper over a few years. If you'd never pay for either, both software packages cost you nothing.

Mats: Same Idea, Different Details

Both systems use sticky mats the same way in practice, so don't overthink this one.

Cricut splits its mats by grip: LightGrip (blue), StandardGrip (green), StrongGrip (purple), and FabricGrip (pink), in 12x12 and 12x24 sizes. One common Cricut complaint is real: mats lose stickiness over time, and you'll re-buy them. Silhouette's light (white), standard (green), and strong tack (purple) mats carry a printed grid, and the larger Cameo 5 Plus models add an electrostatic bed that holds material without adhesive at all.

Practical takeaway: buy the mat your first projects need, and budget for replacements either way.

Materials and Print Then Cut

Good news first: both machines cut the same craft-store vinyl, HTV, cardstock, and iron-on. SVG, DXF, and PNG files cut on both, so whatever cut files you already own travel with you.

The differences are at the edges. Cricut's settings presets favor Cricut-brand material, and Smart Materials, matless cutting with no sticky mat at all, are Cricut-only. Silhouette's Autoblade auto-adjusts blade depth on Cameo machines, which saves you fiddling with a depth dial between materials.

Print then cut is the bigger gap. Cricut caps the printable area around 6.75x9.25 inches on a standard mat (newer machines handle slightly larger). Silhouette's print and cut handles a full 12x12 inches with registration marks, and larger still on bigger Cameos. If you print stickers or full-page designs, that difference shows up on every project.

Machine Lineup and Pricing

At the time of writing, Cricut's lineup runs from the Cricut Joy (~$179, matless with Smart Materials) through the Explore 3 (~$299 MSRP) to the Maker 3 (~$399 MSRP, which takes a knife blade and engraving tools). Street prices often land 15–25% lower on sale, so never pay full MSRP if you can wait a few weeks.

Silhouette counters with the Portrait (~$200) and the Cameo 4/5 (~$300–350, often discounted) — the Portrait undercuts everything Cricut sells.

The pricing isn't the whole story, though. The Maker 3's tooling (knife blade, engraving) goes deeper into materials than any Silhouette blade, but most beginners never touch those tools in year one. Buy for what you make now, and if you're leaning Cricut, our guide to the best Cricut machine for beginners matches each model to a budget.

Which One Should You Buy?

Here's the honest answer: how you work decides, not brand loyalty. Match yourself to a profile:

  • You're a beginner or design on your phone. Cricut wins. Design Space's guided workflow, phone/tablet app, and the biggest ready-made library get you cutting on day one with fewer decisions.
  • You want design freedom and hate subscriptions. Silhouette wins. Free full desktop software, offline cutting over USB, and one-time upgrades instead of a monthly bill. Long-term software cost is genuinely lower.
  • You're money-focused on the machine itself. The Portrait (~$200) is the cheapest capable cutter; the Explore 3 and Cameo trade blows on sale. Check street prices before you commit either way.
  • You print stickers full-page. Silhouette's 12x12 print and cut beats Cricut's ~6.75x9.25 cap.

Neither brand loses outright. Cricut's ecosystem and community are bigger, which means more tutorials, more ready-made files, and more people who can answer your next question. Silhouette's software respects your wallet and your offline time. Both are good machines; the wrong choice only happens when you buy for the brand instead of the workflow.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do SVG files work on both machines?

Yes. Both Cricut and Silhouette cut SVG and DXF files, and PNGs work too. Whatever cut files you buy for one machine will cut on the other.

Is Design Space really free?

The app itself is free to download and use for basic cutting, but most of Cricut's ready-made image library sits behind Cricut Access (around $10/month at the time of writing). Your own SVG uploads cut on the free tier.

Does Silhouette need a subscription?

No. Silhouette Studio's full design tools are free on desktop. Designer Edition and Business Edition are one-time upgrades, not subscriptions.

Which is better for beginners?

Cricut, for most people. The guided Design Space workflow, the phone app, and the biggest ready-made library mean fewer early stumbling blocks. Silhouette is better if you already know desktop design software.

Which is cheaper long-term?

Silhouette, if you'd otherwise pay for Cricut Access. One-time software upgrades beat a monthly subscription over a few years. If you'd never subscribe to either, machine price and mats are the only real cost difference.
