Cricut Maker 5 Projects: What 3mm of Thickness Actually Unlocks

You bought a Cricut to cut thick leather, balsa, and chipboard, and you spent the first month finishing almost every cut with a utility knife. The machine scored the outline, you followed behind it by hand, and the edges came out wobbly enough that the journal cover looked homemade in the bad way.

The old ceiling was real. Stock past roughly 2.4mm either refused to cut through in one pass or tore on the second — chipboard delaminated, leather bowed, balsa snapped along the grain. So you did what everyone does: dropped to thinner material, added a pass, and quietly gave up on the projects you actually wanted to make. That is the gap the Maker 5 was built to close.

Launched September 3, 2026 at $349, the Maker 5 cuts and engraves up to 3mm thick — roughly 25 percent more than the previous generation — so the projects below finally run in a single pass. Here is what is now possible, what is not, and what still works fine on the machine you already own.

What Actually Changed on the Maker 5

Three things matter for real projects. Depth: a 3mm ceiling opens thick vegetable-tanned leather, 3mm balsa, and true bookboard chipboard. Force: the extra pressure holds through dense material instead of stalling mid-cut. Workflow: the knife blade is now the default tool over 2mm, which means fewer test cuts and less guessing. At $349 it sits above the Explore line, so buy it for thick material or engraving — not for vinyl shirts you already cut on an Explore 3.

Thick Leather: Wallets and Journal Covers

Vegetable-tanned leather at 3mm is where this machine earns its price. A wallet back panel, a journal cover with a stamped title, a bag strap blank — all one pass, no knife cleanup. Use the deep-point or knife blade, tape the hide flat with painter’s tape, and cut a test square first because leather varies a lot by tannery. A 12 by 12 inch sheet of 3mm veg-tan runs $18 to $30 and makes two to three journal covers that sell for $45 to $90 finished. Sealing the cut edge with beeswax takes a minute and makes it read as store-bought.

Balsa Wood: Model Kits and Layered Art

Balsa is soft and light, which is exactly why it fails on shallower machines — the blade drags instead of slicing and the sheet crushes. At 3mm, the Maker 5 cuts clean interlocking parts for model planes, ships, and layered shadow boxes. Set the cut to a slower speed and place a scrap of the same thickness under the sheet so the blade does not eat the mat. A 3mm balsa sheet is about $2, and a finished kit that took 40 minutes to cut sells for $25 to $60, painted or unfinished.

Chipboard: Bookboard, Tags, and Box Lids

Thin 0.06 inch chipboard was always usable; 3mm bookboard was not. That difference is the whole rigid gift-box and journal market. Cut box lids, spine boards, and heavy tags that hold their shape, then wrap them in paper or cover material. Use the knife blade, run multiple passes if the density demands it, and watch the mat — bookboard eats adhesive fast, so clean it between sheets. Bookboard costs about $1 per 12 by 12 sheet, and a finished rigid gift box retails for $12 to $20.

Engraving Acrylic, Metal, and Wood

Engraving is the quieter upgrade. The Maker 5 engraves 1/16 inch acrylic, 0.5mm aluminum tags, and hardwood rounds in one setup. Acrylic keychains, pet tags, memorial ornaments, and wedding signs come off the mat with real depth instead of a scratch. Engrave acrylic at a moderate speed and lower pressure, test on a scrap first, and use the mat grid to line up repeat pieces so a run of twenty tags comes out identical.

What Still Works on Older Machines

Be honest about the split. Vinyl, HTV, cardstock, 2mm leather, and 1/16 inch acrylic all run fine on an Explore 3 or an original Maker, and nothing about the Maker 5 makes them better. If your work is shirts, decals, cards, and cake toppers, you do not need this machine. Buy it when the material itself is the reason — thick leather, bookboard, 3mm balsa, or engraving you are doing by hand right now.

FAQ

Is the Cricut Maker 5 worth $349 if I only cut vinyl?

No. Vinyl, HTV, and cardstock run the same on an Explore 3 for less money. The Maker 5 pays off only when you need thickness or engraving.

What is the thickest material the Maker 5 can cut?

Cricut rates it at up to 3mm, which is roughly 25 percent more than the previous generation. That covers 3mm veg-tan leather, 3mm balsa, and 3mm bookboard.

Can I cut 3mm leather on a Maker 3 or an Explore 3?

Not cleanly. Those machines pull thin leather well but stall or tear past about 2mm. For 3mm stock you either upgrade or finish every cut by hand.

Does the Maker 5 engrave metal?

It engraves soft metals like 0.5mm aluminum used for tags and jewelry. It does not cut or engrave steel. Test a scrap first, since coating varies by brand.

Do I need new accessories for the thicker materials?

Use the knife blade for anything over 2mm and a strong grip mat for leather and bookboard. Start with the free SVG pack to prototype shapes on cardstock before you cut expensive leather.
