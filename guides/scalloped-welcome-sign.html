Signage

How to Make the Scalloped Welcome Sign Everyone is Pinning

If you’ve opened Pinterest in the last three months, you’ve seen it: the massive, scalloped-edge welcome sign. It’s dominating weddings, baby showers, and graduation parties.

For a Cricut business, large signage is the holy grail. You can charge $50 to $120 for a physical sign, and the vinyl decal work takes exactly the same effort as a $15 t-shirt.

The Anatomy of the Scalloped Sign

The trick isn't the vinyl lettering; it's the scalloped edge and the minimalist botanical accents. We designed an SVG that gives you the perfect wavy border and a clean, 5-petal blush flower that cuts flawlessly on Oracal 651.

The Build List (Affiliate Links)

You cannot use standard removable vinyl for outdoor signage. It will peel the second the humidity rises.

  • The Vinyl: Oracal 651 Permanent Vinyl (Matte Black)
  • The Accent Colors: Oracal 651 Pastel Assortment (Blush/Sage)
  • The Transfer Tape: HTVRONT Clear Transfer Tape (Medium tack is crucial so you don't pull up the paint on your sign board).
  • The Board: Check your local hardware store, or order Arch/Scalloped Acrylic Blanks.

The Cut-Ready SVG File

Don't spend hours trying to mathematically align scallops in Illustrator. We built the SVG geometry for you. It scales infinitely without losing resolution.
